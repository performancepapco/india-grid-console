GENERATION BY SOURCE MW · 15-min intervals · 00:00–24:00 IST
Each band is one source, stacked to total generation; the dashed line is demand met. Solar swells at midday and vanishes by evening while thermal rises to replace it — the hand-off that sets the evening ramp. In net-load mode the amber trace is demand minus wind and solar: what dispatchable plant must actually serve. The strip below the chart is the demand−generation residual per interval, a check on data consistency and net interchange.
ENERGY MIX MU · day total
Energy, not power: each source's MW integrated over 96 quarter-hours. Thermal holds baseload around the clock, so the energy mix understates solar relative to its midday peak in the stack above.
GRID FREQUENCY Hz vs 50.00 · band
Frequency above 50 Hz means generation exceeds demand; below, demand is winning. The shaded strip is the IEGC operating band. The histogram at right bins every 15-minute sample by frequency, so the distribution's centre and spread are visible alongside the trace.
REGIONAL POSITION five regional grids ·
Regional figures are reported per day, not per interval, so these are energy (MU) and daily peak (MW), not instantaneous shares. West and South carry the wind fleet; East and Northeast report no wind at all and only token solar, so their energy is served almost entirely by thermal and hydro imports. Northern Region has the largest demand and the largest hydro block.

No forecast values are shown until a model supplies them. To feed one, set window.FORECAST to an array of 96 quarter-hour points for the day after the selected day and re-render:
window.FORECAST = [{ d:"2026-09-08", t:"0:15", p:224310, lo:220100, hi:228500 }, … ]
p is predicted demand met in MW; lo/hi the confidence band (optional). Forecast peak reads from max(p); MAPE is computed against actuals once the forecast day exists in the record, so the slots fill themselves.
